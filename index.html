🎉 Latest Release

Say hello to ClipLocal

Most clipboard managers quietly log everything you copy — and many sync it to the cloud. That's fine, until you're copying passwords, tokens, private notes, or client data. ClipLocal was built on a single principle: your clipboard is yours, and it should never leave your Mac.

🔐

Private by design, not as a setting

There's no account to create and no server to trust — because there isn't one. The only time ClipLocal touches the network is a quick version check for updates. Everything else stays on your machine.

🎚️

You decide how much it remembers

Flip to Session-only for sensitive work and history vanishes when you quit. Prefer convenience? Persistent mode keeps your history across restarts — encrypted, and readable only by your macOS account.

🛡️

Passwords stay out of it

Copies marked "concealed" by password managers are skipped automatically, so your secrets never end up in a history list. And a one-click panic wipe is always a click away.

🪶

Light, quiet, and out of your way

ClipLocal lives in your menu bar with no Dock clutter, built natively in Swift with zero dependencies. Turn on Launch at Login and forget it's even there — until you need it.

Installation

⚡ One-Click Terminal Install (Recommended)

The easiest and fastest way to install ClipLocal. Simply copy and paste this command into your Terminal:

/bin/bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arunofhyd/ClipLocal/main/install-cliplocal.command)"

Manual Installation (Fallback)

  1. Download install-cliplocal.command file manually.
  2. Open Terminal (⌘+Space, type "Terminal", press Enter).
  3. Type sh — that's s, h, then a space.
  4. Drag and drop the downloaded file into the Terminal window (its path fills in automatically).
  5. Press Enter and follow the on-screen prompts, then drag ClipLocal onto Applications.
user@macbook ~ % /bin/bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/arunofhyd/ClipLocal/main/install-cliplocal.command)"
▸ Checking for build tools…

First time only: the installer may ask to install Apple's Command Line Tools (a small official Apple download). This lets your Mac build the app locally — which is why macOS trusts it, with no "damaged app" warnings.
